Guide

What are pigeon peas?

Pigeon peas (Cajanus cajan) are a round legume eaten green or dried. Depending on the country they are called gandules, guandules, guandú, quinchoncho or frijol de palo in Spanish, and toor or arhar in India.

What pigeon peas are called in each country

  • Gandul: Peru. Gandules: Puerto Rico.
  • Guandules: Dominican Republic.
  • Guandú: Panama.
  • Quinchoncho: Venezuela.
  • Frijol de palo: a common name across Latin America, including Peru.
  • Toor, tur or arhar: India.

Where do they come from?

Pigeon peas are native to India: genomic studies place their domestication in the center of the country, and India is still the world's largest producer and consumer. They reached the Americas in colonial times and became a Caribbean staple. In Peru they are grown on the northern coast.

Green or dried: what do they taste like?

Green pigeon peas are harvested while the grain is tender: they are light green with a slightly sweet, herbal flavor. Dried pigeon peas are the mature grain, darker and with a stronger flavor.

A drought-tolerant plant

The plant is a deep-rooted shrub that copes well with little water, which is why it is grown in warm, dry areas.

Nutrition

Like other pulses, they provide plant protein and fiber. Pigeon pea seed analyses compiled by the FAO average 22% protein on a dry-matter basis.

How to cook them

Canned pigeon peas are already cooked: just drain them and add them to rice, stews or salads. Dried pigeon peas are soaked and cooked like other dried beans.

Dishes with pigeon peas

  • Arroz con gandules (Puerto Rico).
  • Moro de guandules, with or without coconut (Dominican Republic).
  • Rice with guandú and coconut (Panama).
  • Toor dal and sambar (India).
  • Soups, stews and salads.
